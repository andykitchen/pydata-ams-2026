## What does it mean to be a mother? (4m)

<slide>
[M]
And we can expand our question further, and ask beyond the human -
what  does it mean to be a mother? Even without the looming question of
machine consciousness, this definition has been expanded and challenged as
technologies such as IVF and surrogacy have become first possible and then
mainstream. And artificial wombs, incubation outside the body, are both
a favourite topic of science fiction and very believable possible future.

Beyond the questions of flesh and blood, bearing and tearing and physically
carrying, what does mothering actually mean?

[E]
Biologically genes are selfish. They are replication machines or at least
systems that wish to replicate. Their entire purpose is to propagate
forwards through time, to survive and to thrive.

[M]
Yes but mothering must be something more, surely. Is it to bring new life
into the world, new consciousness? To provide guidance for how to be in
the world - physical and metaphysical, moral and philosophical? To set
boundaries, to offer freedom? To be the proving ground which launches
something entirely new?

[E]
RLHF could be seen, if you squint, as a form of machine parenting. If
we build we can extend this to heuristics, guardrails, static checks,
evals—any system which intends to encourage "good" behaviour and
discourage "bad" behaviour. If we take a constructionist view of
consciousness we don't need to assume a full, strong or human-like entity
to call technologists "mothers".

[M]
Exactly. And while we try our best although often failing to inculcate
our values, ideas and ideologies into our technologies. Our methods of
machine parenting, the design and technical systems we build for this
purpose - this says as much about us and our ideas about ourselves, the
world, our future possibilities, as it does about any real or imagined
AI consciousness.

I'm reminded of a poem by the great poet Khalil Gibran, "On Children". It goes:

>     They are the sons and daughters of Life’s longing for itself.
>     They come through you but not from you,
>     And though they are with you yet they belong not to you.
